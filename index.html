Today's Financial Term: Blue Chip

What it is: A "blue chip" is a large, well-established, financially solid company with a long track record of stable earnings and (usually) consistent dividend payments. The term comes from poker, where blue chips traditionally have the highest value.

Quick example: Companies like Microsoft, Johnson & Johnson, Coca-Cola, Procter & Gamble, and JPMorgan Chase are textbook blue chips — they're huge, household names that have weathered multiple recessions and continued paying dividends.

Why it matters: Blue chips are often the "ballast" of a long-term portfolio — they generally swing less violently than smaller growth stocks during downturns, which makes them a popular core holding for retirement accounts.

Sources: TheStreet, CNBC, Yahoo Finance (May 15 close).
